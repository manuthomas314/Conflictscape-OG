Socio-environmental conflicts in India

Every protest defends a way of life.

ConflictScape follows environmental conflicts across India in which affected people take to the streets — 183 of them so far — what kind of conflict it is, where it happens, and which news outlets report it. Then it asks why, using social practice theory: everyday life is built from things, meanings and know-how, and people resist when those are torn apart.

What counts as a case

A dispute becomes a case when the affected people protest

The project tracks conflicts over land, water, forests, coasts and air where the people who bear the harm organise against it — through marches, blockades, sit-ins, hartals or petitions backed by public action.

Included

  • Place-based environmental conflicts — a mine, a port, a landfill, a protected area
  • Protests against national environmental policy
  • Conflicts where water is at stake, tagged across every type

Left out

  • Labour and privatisation disputes
  • Enforcement against wildlife crime
  • Agricultural market policy, such as the 2021 farm laws movement

Conflict types

Six kinds of conflict, sorted by what they break

Each type is linked to the element of everyday practice it disrupts most. The small three-circle mark on each shows which — the same circles explained in the theory section below.

Wateris not a seventh type. It is a tag that can sit on any conflict, so water disputes stay countable across all six.

    Social practice theory

    Why people protest: the practices behind the conflict

    A practice is something people do again and again — making tea, fishing, farming, gathering from the forest. Social practice theory says a practice is not just a habit inside someone's head. It is held together by three elements that have to be linked, every time it is done.

    When a mine, a highway, a factory or a new rule pulls one of those elements away, the practice can no longer be carried out. Protest is how people defend it.

    The three-element model follows Shove, Pantzar & Watson (2012), The Dynamics of Social Practice: Everyday Life and How It Changes, building on Reckwitz (2002), “Toward a Theory of Social Practices”, European Journal of Social Theory.

    Method

    From a headline to a case on the map

    1. Collect

      News articles from the ten outlets, via Google News RSS and Media Cloud.

    2. Screen

      Keep only stories of environmental conflict where affected people protest.

    3. Classify

      Assign one of the six conflict types, and tag water where it is involved.

    4. Locate

      Match place names against a GeoNames-based gazetteer to find the state.

    5. Explain

      Read each type through materials, meanings and competences.
